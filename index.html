In a world that moves faster every day, we invite you to slow down, tune into your feelings, and just breathe.

Begin with one breath

Why we’re here

Most of us have been running for a long time. Away from what is uncomfortable, toward the next thing, always a little ahead of our own bodies.

Sooner or later the body asks us to stop. This is a place to do exactly that: to sit, to feel what’s here, and to rest in the breath that has been with you all along.

Three gentle invitations

1

Stop

You don’t have to do anything. Let the list wait. Sit down and let the chair or the floor hold you.

Stopping isn’t only quieting the mind. The body has the habit of running too, so let it come to rest as well.

“I have arrived. I am home.”

2

Feel

Come out of the head and into the body. Is there tightness, heaviness, buzzing, warmth, numbness? Let it be here. Don’t push it away, and don’t hold on to it.

If something painful or tender shows up, hold it the way a mother holds a crying baby. You don’t need to understand it. Just stay close, and be gentle.

3

Breathe

The breath is there to rest in. It’s your anchor. There’s nothing to get right. It has been with you since your very first moment, and it’s always here for you.

Feel it come in. Feel it go out. When the mind wanders, simply come back. If discomfort comes up, gently breathe into it.

Room to heal

Your body knows how to heal. An animal that is hurt withdraws into the forest, lies still, and lets its body do the work. It doesn’t strive or fix. It rests.

When you stop, feel and breathe, you do the same: you take care of your body by giving it what it needs. Stopping lets it finally rest. Feeling lets what you’ve been carrying be met with kindness. Breathing brings you home to this moment, again and again. Together they give you and your body room to heal. That’s why we practice.

Rest in the breath

Follow the circle if it helps: in as it grows, out as it softens. Or close your eyes and let your own natural breath do what it already knows how to do.
